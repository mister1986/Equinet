1. Executive Summary

Over the trailing 12 months, equinetapp.com earned real, sustained organic demand — but a rising tide of duplicate and stray URLs is quietly capping how much of that demand converts into clicks.

The site's biggest strength is brand-search dominance: equinet app ranks at position 1.23 with a 50% CTR, and the branded query set (equinet, equinet app, common misspellings) accounts for the large majority of all clicks. Farriers who already know the app find it instantly.

The two things holding EQUINET back aren't lack of demand — they're structural. First, average ranking position degraded from ~6.3–7.4 (Sep 2025–Mar 2026) to a low of 10.8 in August 2026, even as impression volume held roughly steady — a pattern consistent with Google's March 2026 core update, which rewarded topical authority and penalized thin/duplicate content more heavily. Second, the audit found that at least 100 of the 220 URLs Google has indexed for this domain (~46%) are duplicates, tracking-parameter variants, or stale test pages competing against the real pages for the same rankings (see the breakdown diagram in Section 5).

Third, a cluster of 15+ high-commercial-intent "farrier software" keywords — farrier scheduling software, farrier CRM, farrier invoicing software, farrier business management — are already generating meaningful impression volume but sit on page 2 (positions 10–22) and earn zero clicks. This is the single largest untapped growth lever the data points to.

Overall assessment: Needs Work

Strong brand foundation and genuinely useful feature content, but real technical debt (duplicate URL structure, no structured data, thin AEO/GEO framing) is actively suppressing non-branded growth. None of the fixes below require a rebuild — they're config, consolidation, and content-depth work.

Top 3 priorities:

  • Consolidate the URL structure — resolve the root vs. /us/ duplication, strip HubSpot tracking parameters from indexable URLs, and retire orphaned/legacy pages.
  • Build for the "farrier software" keyword cluster — dedicated, comprehensive landing pages for the commercial-intent terms currently stuck at positions 10–22.
  • Add structured data and answer-first content — Search Console shows zero rich-result appearances beyond automatic translation; there is no FAQ, Organization, or Article schema currently earning enhanced SERP real estate.

Based on a live fetch of the homepage, the Features hub, an individual feature page (Horse Profiles), and the blog index.

Pages with real visibility but the weakest pull-through — the gap between the grey (impressions, scaled ÷100) and rust (clicks) bars is the opportunity.

Why this matters

/features/horseentries pulled 1,842 impressions this year and converted exactly one click. That's not a ranking problem — it's already showing up in results. It's a title-and-snippet problem: the page isn't telling searchers it answers their question before they click.

Fixing the title/meta on these five pages alone, with no ranking improvement at all, could plausibly recover several hundred clicks a year just from closing the CTR gap to the site average (2.91%).

Other index bloat found in the export

  • HubSpot tracking-parameter URLs indexed separately — 31 URLs carrying ?__hstc=…&__hssc=…&__hsfp=… query strings are showing up as distinct pages rather than being canonicalized back to the clean URL.
  • Case-sensitivity duplicates — /US/CONTACT and a matching path are indexed separately from their lowercase equivalents.
  • Orphaned legacy Tilda pages — five auto-generated slugs (/page90840006.html and similar) are still indexed with no apparent navigation path pointing to them.
  • Test/staging pages left live — /test3, /testhorseowner, /old, and an expired /us/sweepstakes promotion are all still indexed and receiving impressions.

Structured data & rich results

The Search Console "Search appearance" report shows exactly one non-standard appearance type across the full 12 months: automatic translated results (1 click / 50 impressions). There is no FAQPage, Organization, Article, or Breadcrumb rich result showing anywhere in the data — consistent with the live page fetches, which surfaced no visible structured-data markup. This means the site isn't eligible for the FAQ snippets, sitelinks, or enhanced listings that would meaningfully lift CTR on pages like the FAQ and feature pages.

Checked directly, but couldn't be fully verified in this session

robots.txt and sitemap.xml could not be fetched directly in this audit. Given the URL bloat found above, confirm with your developer or in Search Console → Settings → Sitemaps that: (1) a sitemap exists and lists only canonical URLs, (2) canonical tags are present and correct on the /us/ and parameterized variants, and (3) Core Web Vitals are passing in PageSpeed Insights — this session couldn't measure real-user performance data.

6. Answer & Generative Engine Optimization

How ready this content is to be lifted into a featured snippet, an AI Overview, or a direct citation from ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Gemini.

What's working

Testimonials name real farriers with real credentials — Tim Shannon, CJF, AWCF; Loren Altman, CF; Eva Clark, APF‑III — exactly the kind of quotable, credentialed, first-hand-experience signal generative engines weight when deciding what to trust. It's currently confined to the homepage and success-story pages rather than woven into the blog and feature content where it would do more AEO/GEO work.

What's missing

No FAQ schema, no question-phrased headings, and no "answer-first" paragraphs on the feature pages — each one opens with a benefit statement, not a direct answer to a real query like "how do I schedule farrier appointments by shoeing cycle?" No visible author or dateModified on blog content weakens both freshness signals and citability.

Recommended pattern

For each feature page, open the detail section with a direct 2–3 sentence answer before the current marketing copy, and add 3–4 genuine FAQ entries with FAQPage schema. Example for the Appointments feature:

Q: How does EQUINET schedule farrier appointments?
EQUINET schedules appointments around each horse's shoeing cycle and sends automated SMS and email invites with built-in RSVP tracking, so farriers always have a live view of who's confirmed for the week.

This single change — repeated across the eight feature pages and the FAQ page — is the most direct route to both classic featured snippets and AI Overview citations, and it costs no new content strategy, only restructuring of what the team already knows.

8. International Demand & Brand Notes

EQUINET is only available in Australia, New Zealand, and the United States — but a meaningful share of search demand is coming from markets it doesn't yet serve.

Impressions (grey, left axis) vs. CTR (rust dots, right axis) by country. Canada and the UK stand out: high visibility, weak conversion — consistent with a market EQUINET doesn't yet serve.

Canada: 15,835 impressions, 0.34% CTR

Canada is the site's second-largest source of impressions after the US — larger than Australia — yet converts at a tenth of the US click-through rate. This is either a real expansion signal worth validating with the product team, or, at minimum, a reason to make availability clearer in the meta description so unqualified clicks (and the CTR drag that comes with them) drop.

One brand-monitoring note: the query export includes a small number of impressions pairing a testimonial farrier's name with unrelated adult-content search terms — almost certainly an unrelated third party sharing that name rather than anything on the EQUINET site itself. Not actionable from an SEO standpoint, but worth a quick periodic glance in Search Console's query list if the team uses full names in future testimonials.
